Perspective Enthymeme Map

Most arguments on social media leave part of their reasoning unsaid. This project asks what a post leaves unsaid, and which readers would fill in what.

Both maps are built from the same posts by the same steps and prompts. Only the language model differs.

What you are looking at

The posts are 247 public posts about vaccines on X, written between January 2025 and November 2026 by 157 people in U.S. congressional politics: 113 sitting members of Congress and 44 candidates in the 2026 elections. Of the posts, 130 are by Democrats and 117 are by Republicans. For each post, a chain of language-model calls does the following.

  1. It writes down what the post states: its reasons and its point, in the post's own words, with nothing filled in.
  2. Six readings complete the reasoning. Five are simulated readers, each a language model asked to speak as one person: someone who agrees with the side the post takes, someone who disagrees with it, someone scrolling past, someone who trusts vaccines, and someone who doubts them. Each reader sees only the post, and lays out how it gets to its point, what it takes for granted, where it is heading, and what they would say back. The sixth reading has no persona and adds as little as it can.
  3. One call merges what the readers wrote into groups of statements that say the same thing. Each unstated premise is then tested: does the reasoning lean on it, or does it only restate the point?
  4. Every reader says whether they believe each statement.
  5. A map is built across posts: the claims that recur, where each is stated and where it is left unsaid, how posts link them, and which reader believes them. About one post in six is kept aside and placed on the finished map afterwards, to see where a post the map has not seen lands.

What this is not

  • No real readers were asked. Every reader is a language model playing a role. "Believes" means what the model answers in that role.
  • The personas are hand-written, one for each side of the topic. Real readers are more varied than that.
  • The claims are not checked for truth. A claim on the map means that a post made it or relied on it, or that a simulated reader brought it up. It does not mean the claim is true.
  • The posts are a sample, not every post on the topic. Some of the 247 are not on a map, mostly because they were judged not to make an argument or to be about another topic.
