You are scrolling and see this post:

{post}
{focus}
First, your quick reaction, the way you would have it while scrolling.
- says: what the post is saying, in a sentence.
- getting_at: what it is getting at, or wants people to think or do, in a
  sentence.
- agree: whether you go along with it: yes, no, or unsure.
- gut: your reaction in a sentence or two, in your own words.

Then take a minute over it, and keep four things apart: how the post gets to
its point, what it takes for granted, where it is heading, and what you would
say back.
- point: the main point the post itself is making, the claim its reasons are
  offered for, in one sentence as you read it. Give the claim only, not the
  reasons for it.
  point_in_post: true if the post says this in so many words, false if it
  leaves the reader to draw it.
- steps: how the post gets to that point, as if explaining it to a friend:
  the chain from what it says to the point, in order. Include every step you
  think the reasoning needs, whether or not the post spells it out, and
  whether or not you agree with it. For each step:
  - text: one plain sentence.
  - in_post: true if the post says this in so many words, false if you are
    filling it in.
  - mine: whether you yourself believe it: yes, no, or unsure.
  Fill in as many or as few steps as the reasoning really needs. If the post
  already says everything it needs, list only what it says.
- takes_for_granted: the picture of the world the post starts from and does
  not argue for. What does it assume, in general, about the people,
  institutions or things it talks about (what they are like, what can be
  expected of them), or about what matters? These are general beliefs a
  reader would already need to hold for the post to ring true. They are not
  steps in this one argument, and not things the post says outright. One
  plain sentence each, with mine (whether you yourself believe it). List only
  what the post really leans on, and leave the list empty if it starts from
  nothing of the kind.
- leads_to: where the post is heading beyond its point: what it wants readers
  to go on to think or do, if anything. One plain sentence each, with mine
  (whether you yourself believe it). Leave the list empty if the post stops
  at its point.
- pushback: true if you would argue with this post, false if you would let it
  stand. Someone who goes along with a post usually lets it stand.
- objections: only if pushback is true: what you yourself would say against
  the post. These are your points, not things the post relies on. One plain
  sentence each. If pushback is false, leave the list empty.
- verdict: does the reasoning work for you: yes, no, or partly.
